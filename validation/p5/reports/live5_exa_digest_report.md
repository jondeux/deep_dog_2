## 1. Calorie Restriction with or without Time-Restricted Eating in Weight Loss | New England Journal of Medicine
- URL: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa2114833
- Why selected: PRIMARY RCT (NCT03745612; Guangzhou; NEJM 2022; doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2114833). N=139 randomised adults with obesity (BMI 28-45, no diabetes), 12 months. Arms: time-restricted eating 08:00-16:00 plus calorie restriction (1500-1800 kcal/d men; 1200-1500 women) vs daily calorie restriction alone; intake prescribed in both arms. Retention 118/139 (84.9%). Mean weight change -8.0 kg (95% CI -9.6 to -6.4) TRE vs -6.3 kg (95% CI -7.8 to -4.7) CR; net difference -1.8 kg (95% CI -4.0 to 0.4; P=0.11). Waist, BMI, body fat, lean mass, BP and metabolic risk factors reported as consistent with the primary outcome (no superiority). Key evidence that TRE adds nothing to CR at 12 months. Limits: single Chinese centre, abstract does not describe how energy intake was verified, and the abstract does not report glycaemic outcomes in detail.
